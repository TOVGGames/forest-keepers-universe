# Forest Keepers, talking format, episode 2: "The Flower That Wouldn't Open" (script)

**Status (2026-11-17): script for the owner. Nothing voiced, nothing rendered. Planned slot: Sat Dec 5 (a week after episode 1); voicing and the render start inside the 10-day window, from Nov 25.** The owner picked this story for episode
2 on 2026-11-17 ("Blossom's origin"). The format follows `forest_keepers_talk_handoff.md`, with his notes on episode 1:
Springs a little quieter, and a fade instead of watching a Keeper walk off the screen.

- **Lesson:** some things open in their own time. You can't hurry someone who is shy or new. The kindest thing is to
  be gentle and stay with them until they're ready.
  - Springs tries to wake the bud with a big loud song, and it shuts tighter.
  - Digs finds nothing wrong underneath: it just isn't ready.
  - The Keepers stay with it all night and talk to it softly. In the morning it opens, and Blossom says she could
    come out because someone told her she didn't have to.
- **Nature facts** (checked, sources below):
  - many flowers close at night and open again when the morning sun warms them
  - the daisy's name comes from "day's eye", because it opens with the day
  - mole tunnels let air and rainwater down to plant roots
  - some flowers open at dusk instead, like the evening primrose, and moths visit them at night
  - bees can see colours we can't, and many petals have patterns, invisible to us, that guide bees to the middle
- **Cast:** Sprout, Springs, Digs and Glo, with the narrator Johanna. **New: Blossom** (chapter 6). No Wheels, Twigs,
  Hans or Melody.
- **Length:** six chapters, about 8.4 minutes (see Numbers).
- **Promise -> payoff:** the title promises a flower that won't open.
  - Chapter 1 sets it up: Glo saw a bud glowing in the night.
  - It stays shut through chapters 2-5 (a song, a look underground, a whole night of waiting).
  - In chapter 6 it opens in the morning sun, and Blossom comes out of it.
- **Pronouns:** Sprout is "it"; Glo gets no pronoun; Blossom is "she".
- **It follows straight on from episode 1,** which ended "Tomorrow. Tonight, you sleep." This is that tomorrow, their
  first day as Forest Keepers.

**Choices for the owner** (the script works however he answers):
1. **The glow.** In Blossom's design the blue light came from an orb in her flower. With no orbs, the bud itself glows
   a soft, even light blue (the look he approved for her sleeping bud in round 3). Once she's awake, the glow is gone.
   From then on her flower shows how she feels, as designed.
2. **Her voice.** She needs her own ElevenLabs voice: a short audition (3-4 gentle, young, storybook voices; never
   Lindsay). He picks.
3. **Her look in the flat talk style.** One look sheet before anything is voiced. It keeps his design rounds: pink
   cherry wood, a darker bark-petal hem, no legs (she floats), the flower on her head that opens and closes with her
   feelings, the "little flick" lashes that ride down with her eyelids, and a glitter cloud from the whole hem only
   when she flies fast (she doesn't fly fast in this episode).
4. Anything Toby wants changed in how she arrives goes first.

**How it moves** (the format's rules, plus his episode 1 notes):
- Nobody picks up, carries or builds anything.
- Walks are short. Springs hops; Glo and Blossom float.
- Anyone travelling comes toward the camera or across it, never away.
- A Keeper's size comes from where its feet are.
- Characters never overlap; when two are close, a gap stays between them.
- **Leaving a place is a turn, a step or a hop, and a fade.** Nobody walks out of the frame. A new place fades in
  with everyone already there.
- Springs is 1 dB under the others (3 dB under episode 1).
- Delivery notes in brackets (whispering, softly, the chant) become v4 audio tags in the takes, and each take is
  listened for them; they are never read out.
- Music is the original theme in its original key. It stays down under talk and comes up slowly only in the real
  silences: the opening, the bud shutting, the roots, the sunset, the night, the bud opening, the end.

## 1. The First Morning (sunrise, under the old oak, about 75 s)

*The old oak at the edge of the forest, where episode 1 ended. First light. Sprout, Springs and Digs are asleep among
the roots with gaps between them; Glo hovers beside them, lantern low. The theme plays over the wide shot.*

**Narrator:** The next morning, the sun came up over the forest. And for the very first time, it shone on the Forest
Keepers.

*Springs' eyes pop open.*

**Springs:** Morning! Morning, everybody! Is it tomorrow yet?

**Digs:** It's tomorrow. ...It's very bright tomorrow.

**Sprout:** Our first day as Forest Keepers! What do Forest Keepers do first?

**Glo:** First, a keeper looks. And listens. And notices things.

**Springs:** I notice... that I'm hungry.

**Digs:** I notice that Springs is hungry. Every morning.

**Glo:** Then here's something I noticed. Last night, while you were all asleep, I saw a little blue light. Far across
the meadow, under the old cherry tree.

**Sprout:** A blue light? Like yours?

**Glo:** Not like mine. Softer. It was coming from a flower bud. And it was glowing.

**Springs:** A glowing flower! Let's go, let's go!

**Narrator:** And so the Forest Keepers set off on their very first job: to find out about a flower that glowed in the
dark.

*Springs turns and hops once to the right; the others turn after him. Fade.*

## 2. The Bud under the Cherry Tree (morning, about 80 s)

*The far edge of the meadow. An old cherry tree in pink blossom; a few petals drift down. In the short grass at its
foot sits a big, round, closed bud, as tall as Sprout, wrapped tight. It is one soft, even light blue all over, and
glows faintly even in the daylight. Beside the tree, to the right, is a fresh molehill. Fade in: the Keepers are
already there, in an arc to the sides of the bud and in front of it, with gaps between them: Glo hovering on the left,
Sprout left of the bud, Springs in front a little to the right, Digs on the right by the molehill.*

**Sprout:** There it is.

**Springs:** It's so big! Flowers aren't usually that big. Are they?

**Sprout:** What do you think is inside?

**Springs:** A giant bug! No, a giant bee! No... a giant frog!

**Digs:** Frogs don't come out of flowers, Springs.

**Springs:** You don't know that. Nobody's ever looked!

**Digs:** ...That's fair.

*Digs lifts his nose and sniffs. His whiskers twitch.*

**Digs:** It smells like the cherry tree. And like rain. And like... something new. I don't know that smell.

**Glo:** It was closed all night. I thought it would open in the morning.

**Narrator:** Many flowers close up at night, and open again in the morning, when the sun warms them. The daisy got
its name that way. Long ago, people called it the "day's eye", because it opens with the day.

**Sprout:** But the sun's up now. And it's still closed.

**Springs:** Maybe it's still asleep! Like I was, all winter. It just needs someone to wake it up.

**Digs:** Springs...

**Springs:** Leave it to me! I know the best wake-up song in the whole pond!

**Sprout:** Is it a quiet song?

**Springs:** It's a wonderful song.

*Springs puffs himself up. Cut.*

## 3. The Wake-Up Song (morning, about 60 s)

*Closer, the same place, everyone where they were: the bud in the middle, Springs in front of it a little to the
right. Springs takes a huge breath. (The theme drops out completely under the chant, so its tune can't clash.)*

**Springs** (a bouncy chant, cheerful, not shouted; at his lowered level): Wake up, wake up, the sun is up! Ribbit, ribbit, open up! Wake up, wake up...

*The bud squeezes tighter. Its glow goes dim. A small shiver runs through its petals. Silence (music up a little).*

**Sprout:** Springs! Springs, wait. Look.

**Springs:** ...It closed up even tighter.

**Sprout:** I think it got scared.

*Springs' smile goes small.*

**Springs:** I was only trying to help.

**Glo:** You were. And that was kind. But sometimes, helping means being a little quieter.

**Springs:** Quieter. ...I'm not very good at quieter.

**Sprout:** But you're good at hellos. Yesterday, when I heard you croak, I came straight down the hill to find you.

**Springs:** You did, didn't you?

**Glo:** Some of us like a big hello. And some of us need a small one.

**Digs:** Maybe it's not asleep. Maybe something's wrong underneath. I'll go and have a look. Down where nobody sees.

*Digs turns toward a molehill beside the tree. Fade.*

## 4. Under the Ground (morning, about 70 s)

*Under the meadow: Digs' tunnel, dark brown earth, little stones, tree roots hanging down. In the middle, the bud's
own roots reach down from above, and they glow the same soft blue. Fade in: Digs is already there, beside the roots;
the lamp on his hard hat lights the tunnel around him. When Sprout or Springs speaks, it cuts up to the meadow:
the two of them beside the molehill, looking down into it, everyone keeping their place. Digs answers in the tunnel
shot.*

**Narrator:** Under the meadow, Digs knew every tunnel. And under the cherry tree, he found the roots of the glowing
bud.

**Digs:** Hello, roots. Let's see how you are.

*Digs sniffs along the roots. His whiskers twitch.*

**Digs:** Strong. Not too wet, not too dry. Nice warm soil all around.

**Narrator:** Moles help the soil without even trying. Their tunnels let air and rainwater down to the roots of the
plants.

**Sprout** (up in the meadow, at the molehill): Digs? Is it sick?

**Digs:** No. It's as healthy as anything. Its glow goes all the way down here. Nothing's wrong with it at all.

**Sprout** (at the molehill): Don't you get lonely down there, Digs?

**Digs:** Not for me. Down here it's quiet, and nobody's in a hurry. Roots don't rush. They just grow, a little bit
every day.

**Springs** (at the molehill): Then why won't it open?

*Digs looks up at the roots for a long moment.*

**Digs:** I think... it's just not ready.

*Silence (music up). The roots glow softly, brighter and dimmer, slowly, like breathing.*

**Digs:** Take your time, little one. Your roots are fine.

*Fade.*

## 5. Waiting (sunset into night, under the cherry tree, about 95 s)

*The cherry tree and the bud again, wide. All four sit in an arc to the sides of the bud and in front of it, with gaps
between them and room left beside it, Glo hovering just above the grass. The sun is low.*

**Narrator:** All day, the Keepers waited. The sun went across the sky. And the bud stayed closed.

**Springs:** Maybe we should try again. A little louder?

**Digs:** No.

**Glo:** No, Springs.

**Springs:** ...A little softer?

**Sprout:** When I woke up under the leaves, I'd waited all winter first. Until the ground was warm. Nobody could
make me come up any faster.

**Glo:** Some things open in their own time.

**Springs:** But what if it's lonely in there? All by itself?

**Glo:** Then we stay. Nobody should be alone when they're new.

**Springs:** I'm still hungry. But I'm staying.

**Springs:** Were you ever lonely, Glo?

**Glo:** Sometimes the nights were very long. I'm glad I'm not by myself now.

**Springs:** Well, now somebody's sitting with everybody.

*Silence (music up). The sun goes down. The sky turns pink, then deep blue, and the stars come out. In the dark, the
bud's blue glow looks brighter. Glo's lantern glows warm beside it.*

**Narrator:** Some flowers wait for the night. The evening primrose opens at dusk, and moths come to visit it in the
dark. But this bud was waiting for something else.

*Sprout takes one small step sideways, closer to the bud, and stops, still a little way from it.*

**Sprout:** Hello in there. You don't have to come out. We'll just be here.

**Sprout:** I'm Sprout. That's Digs, and Glo, and Springs. We're the Forest Keepers. We only started today.

**Springs** (whispering): This is my quietest voice. Hello.

*The bud's glow brightens softly, once, then again.*

**Glo:** It's listening.

**Digs:** Good night, little bud.

*Far away, the spring peepers sing. Springs, Digs and Sprout fall asleep where they sit, one by one. Glo's light
stays on beside the bud.*

**Glo:** I'll keep watch. Not by myself.

*Fade.*

## 6. Blossom (sunrise, under the cherry tree, about 120 s)

*First light. The Keepers asleep around the bud, Glo awake beside it. The sun comes up over the meadow, and its light
moves across the grass to the bud.*

**Narrator:** And in the morning, after a whole night with friends beside it... the bud began to open.

*Silence (music up). Slowly, the outer petals unfold, one after another, and turn pink and bark-brown as they open:
they become a hem of bark petals. Inside, curled up, is someone: pink cherry wood, eyes shut, a small flower on top of
her head, closed tight. The blue glow fades, from the petals and from the ground under her, into the sunlight. She
opens her eyes. She lets go of the ground, lifts gently, and floats just above the grass. The others wake, one by
one.*

**Blossom** (softly, shy): ...Hello?

**Sprout:** Hello! You opened!

**Blossom:** I heard you. All night. Somebody said I didn't have to come out.

**Sprout:** That was me.

**Blossom:** That's why I could.

*The flower on her head opens, just a little.*

**Springs** (whispering): Hello. I'm the quiet one.

**Blossom:** You're the one with the big song.

**Springs:** ...That was me too. Sorry.

**Blossom:** It was a nice song. It was just very... big.

*She laughs, a small laugh. Her flower opens halfway.*

**Glo:** Look at your flower. It opens when you're happy.

**Blossom:** It does? ...It does!

*Her flower opens wide, with tiny gold sparkles.*

**Digs:** What's your name?

**Blossom:** I don't know. I only just opened.

**Sprout:** Somebody gave me my name. Can I give you one? You look just like the cherry blossoms. ...Blossom?

**Blossom:** Blossom. ...I like it.

*A bee hums in and circles her flower.*

**Narrator:** Bees can see colors that we can't. Many flowers have patterns on their petals that we can't see at
all, and they show the bees the way to the middle.

**Blossom:** It tickles!

*The bee hums away.*

**Blossom:** What do Forest Keepers do?

**Glo:** We look after the forest. And each other.

**Blossom:** Then can I help? I'd like to make everything beautiful.

**Sprout:** You can help me with the flowers!

**Springs:** Yes! Welcome to the Forest Keepers! ...Quietly welcome.

**Narrator:** And that is how Blossom came to the Forest Keepers. Some things open in their own time. And the kindest
thing a friend can do is wait with them, gently, until they're ready.

*The theme comes up. The five under the cherry tree, petals drifting down. Hold. End sign: what should the Forest
Keepers do next?*

## Facts and sources

- Flowers closing at night and opening with the morning warmth (tulips and crocuses open with warmth and close as
  it cools): [BBC Science Focus](https://www.sciencefocus.com/nature/why-do-poppy-flowers-open-in-the-morning-and-close-at-night),
  van Doorn and van Meeteren, "Flower opening and closure: a review", *J. Exp. Botany* 54 (2003).
  The line says "many flowers".
- Daisy from Old English *dæges eage*, "day's eye", because it opens at dawn and closes at dusk:
  [Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/Daisy).
- Mole tunnels improve aeration and let water percolate down: [Virginia Tech Extension](https://pubs.ext.vt.edu/420/420-201/420-201.html),
  [UConn Extension](https://ipm-cahnr.media.uconn.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3216/2022/03/UConn-Extension-Mole-Management-2022.pdf).
- Evening primrose opens at dusk and is pollinated mainly by hawk moths:
  [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/evening-primrose-blooming-dusk), [CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/asmagazine/2022/05/24/evening-primroses-show-complexities-floral-fragrance).
- Bees see ultraviolet; many petals carry ultraviolet "nectar guides" invisible to people that lead bees to the
  center: [US Forest Service](https://www.fs.usda.gov/wildflowers/pollinators/Plant_Strategies/visualcues.shtml),
  [Horth et al., wild bees and Rudbeckia UV guides](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4001241/).
- Cherry trees blossom in spring, the same season as episode 1 (sprouting acorns, spring peepers). Pink blossom is a
  cherry, not a wild cherry (wild cherries flower white), so the script just says "cherry tree".
- Blossom coming out of a glowing bud is the story's magic (the owner's origin for her, 2026-11-14: "a magical flower
  the Keepers find turns into her"), not a fact.
