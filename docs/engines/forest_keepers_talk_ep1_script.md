# Forest Keepers, talking format, episode 1: "How the Forest Keepers Began" (script)

**Status (2026-11-17): script for the owner. Nothing voiced, nothing rendered.** The owner, 2026-11-17: "kickstart
their origin from the beginning in the new format". The format follows `forest_keepers_talk_handoff.md`. A fresh
reviewer went through it once against the format rules, the facts and the story, and its notes are fixed here.

- **Lesson:** you don't have to be the same as your friends to belong with them. Friends look after each other.
  - Sprout finds out it is a little oak tree, so it can't hop, dig or glow, and it worries a tree ends up alone.
  - The old oak shows it otherwise: a tree is home to everyone, and everyone looks after it.
- **Nature facts** (checked, sources below):
  - some acorns wait under the leaves all winter
  - some frogs rest on the pond bottom all winter, breathing through their skin
  - moles see very little and find their way by touch and smell
  - an oak's roots spread out wide
  - jays and squirrels bury acorns, and the forgotten ones can grow into oaks
  - an old oak is home to hundreds of kinds of creatures
  - spring peepers sing on spring nights
- **Cast:** Sprout, Springs (the frog), Digs and Glo, with the narrator Johanna. No Wheels, Twigs, Hans, Blossom or
  Melody.
- **Length:** six chapters, about 8.7 minutes (see Numbers).
- **Promise -> payoff:** the title promises how the Forest Keepers began.
  - The first line sets it up: "Before there were any Forest Keepers...".
  - In chapter 6 the four choose what each looks after, and name themselves.
- **Sprout is "it"** (owner 2026-11-03). Glo gets no pronoun.

**Choices for the owner** (the script works either way he answers):
1. **Where the Keepers come from.** The old origin was the factory ("trees in, monsters out"). The talking format has
   no building, and Melody probably goes. So here the Keepers are forest creatures:
   - Sprout is an acorn that sprouts under the old oak.
   - Springs is a frog waking from his winter at the bottom of the pond.
   - Digs is a mole.
   - Glo has kept watch over the forest at night, alone, for a long time. What Glo is stays a gentle mystery.
2. **Digs' hard hat** isn't explained without the factory. Children won't mind. It could also go.

**How it moves** (the format's rules):
- Nobody picks up, carries or builds anything.
- Walks are short. Springs hops, Glo floats.
- Anyone travelling comes toward the camera or across it, never away.
- A Keeper's size comes from where its feet are.
- A change of place is a cut, and everyone keeps their place across it.
- Exits and entrances match: whoever leaves on the right comes in from the left of the next place.
- Characters never overlap: when two are close, a gap stays between them.
- Grass is short, below their feet.
- Sprout's seedling leaves fold, droop and lift by bending at the stem, never by stretching.
- Music is the original theme in its original key. It stays down under talk and comes up slowly only in the real
  silences: the opening, the sun on the acorn, the walks, the sunset, the peepers, the end.

## 1. Under the Old Oak (early spring morning, about 70 s)

*The edge of the forest: tall trees behind, and in front of them, at one side, a huge old oak whose roots reach into
a meadow of last year's leaves. Near us, among the damp leaves, a little green acorn stands with its eyes shut and its
two seedling leaves folded down. The theme plays over the wide shot.*

**Narrator:** Before there were any Forest Keepers, there was a forest. And at its edge stood an old oak tree, and
one little acorn, asleep under the leaves.

**Narrator:** Some acorns wait under the leaves all winter. And this one waited, until spring came and warmed the
ground.

*Silence (music up). Sunlight comes across the leaves to the acorn. Its eyes open. Its two seedling leaves lift
up, slowly, and open toward the light.*

**Sprout:** Oh! ...Hello?

**Sprout:** Hello? Is anybody there?

*Birdsong. Nobody answers.*

**Sprout:** The leaves are all soft and wet. And the air smells like rain.

**Sprout:** I'm awake. I'm green. And I have leaves on my head. But what am I? And where do I belong?

*A loud, happy croak from down the hill, off to the right. Sprout looks toward it.*

**Sprout:** Somebody's down there!

*Sprout walks a few steps to the right, out of the shot. Cut.*

## 2. The Pond (morning, about 100 s)

*The pond's edge: reeds, lily pads, a muddy bank. Springs sits on the bank near us, right of centre, and gives a huge
yawn, then blinks at the sky. Sprout walks in from the left and stops, a step away from him.*

**Narrator:** Down at the pond, someone else was waking up too.

**Springs:** Good morning, sun! Good morning, sky! Good morning... um. Who are you?

**Sprout:** I don't know. I just woke up.

**Springs:** Me too! I've been asleep at the bottom of the pond all winter.

**Sprout:** Under the water? How did you breathe?

**Springs:** Through my skin! When the water's cold and I'm all slowed down, that's all I need.

**Narrator:** It's true. Some frogs rest at the bottom of a pond all winter long, breathing through their skin, and
waiting for spring.

**Springs:** Hey, you've got a little sprout on your head! A little sprout... Sprout! Can I call you Sprout?

**Sprout:** Sprout. ...I like it.

**Springs:** And I'm Springs! Because I'm full of hops. Watch!

*Springs hops once, left to right along the bank, in the middle of the shot, and lands. Sprout tries a little bounce
on the spot. Its feet hardly leave the ground.*

**Sprout:** I don't think I'm a hopper.

**Springs:** Are you a frog?

**Sprout:** I don't think I'm a frog either.

**Springs:** Then let's find out what you are! I know someone who knows everything about this meadow. Well...
everything under it.

**Narrator:** So off they went together. Springs went first, of course.

*Springs hops out to the right, and Sprout walks after him. Cut.*

## 3. The Molehills (late morning, about 105 s)

*The meadow, dotted with little hills of fresh soil. Digs sits beside one of them, right of centre, in his hard hat,
squinting. Springs hops in from the left, and Sprout walks in after him, stopping on Digs' left with a gap between
them.*

**Springs:** Digs! Digs, we have a question!

**Digs:** I'm awake, I'm awake. Who's that with you? I can't see very well up here. It's too bright.

**Sprout:** I'm Sprout. I'm new. And I don't know what I am.

**Narrator:** Moles have tiny eyes, and they see very little. Under the ground, they find their way by touch, and by
smell.

**Digs:** I mostly live down there. Tunnels and tunnels. It's cool, and dark, and it smells wonderful. Come a little
closer.

*Sprout takes one step closer and stops, still a little way from him. Digs lifts his nose and sniffs the air. His
whiskers twitch.*

**Digs:** You smell like the old oak tree.

**Sprout:** The big one at the edge of the forest?

**Digs:** I know its roots very well. They spread out, and out, under the whole meadow. And you smell just like them.

**Sprout:** But that tree is so big. And I'm so small.

**Digs:** Every oak tree starts out as one small acorn. Even that one.

**Sprout:** So... I'm a tree?

**Digs:** A very little one. With feet.

**Springs:** A tree with feet!

*Sprout turns its head and looks up and away, out of the shot, toward the old oak. It goes quiet. Its seedling leaves
bend down a little.*

**Sprout:** But I can't hop like you. And I can't dig like Digs. And trees just stand in one place. All by themselves.

**Digs:** ...Huh.

*Digs and Springs look at each other. Cut.*

## 4. The Meadow at Sunset (about 100 s)

*The three sit in short grass on a gentle slope as the sun goes down: Springs on the left, Sprout in the middle, Digs
on the right, a little apart. Sprout's seedling leaves still droop.*

**Narrator:** All afternoon, Sprout was quiet. Even Springs couldn't think of anything to say. And that almost never
happens.

**Springs:** Sprout? You don't have to be a tree today. You can just be Sprout.

**Sprout:** But if I'm a tree, one day I'll have to stay in one place. And you'll hop away. And Digs will dig away.

**Digs:** I wouldn't dig that far.

**Springs:** And I'd hop back. I always hop back. Ask anybody.

**Sprout:** Promise?

**Springs:** Frog's honor.

*Silence (music up). The sun goes down behind the hills. The sky turns pink, then deep blue, and the first stars
come out. A soft light moves in from the left: Glo floats in, lantern glowing, and settles a little in front of
the others, nearer to us.*

**Glo:** Hello, everyone. I heard someone new down here.

**Springs:** Glo! This is Sprout. Sprout woke up today.

**Glo:** Welcome, Sprout. I'm Glo. I keep watch over the forest at night.

**Sprout:** All by yourself?

**Glo:** For a long time, yes. Everyone was asleep while I was awake.

**Sprout:** Wasn't that lonely?

**Glo:** Sometimes. But I'll tell you a secret. I was never really alone. Come with me. I'll show you.

*Cut.*

## 5. Night under the Old Oak (night, about 110 s)

*The old oak at the edge of the forest, under the stars. Sprout, Springs and Digs stand among the roots, which stay
behind them all through the shot. Glo hovers beside them, and her lantern lights the trunk. From far away comes a
chorus of tiny peeping voices.*

**Springs:** Hear that? Those are my cousins! The spring peepers!

**Narrator:** Spring peepers are tiny frogs. On spring nights, they sing all together, so loudly you can hear them
from far away.

*Silence (music up a little, under the peepers). Glo's lantern brightens on the branches above.*

**Glo:** Look up, Sprout. What do you see?

**Sprout:** A nest, with birds asleep in it. And a squirrel, curled up in a hole in the trunk. And little moths on
the bark.

**Digs:** And down below, its roots hold the soil together, all around my tunnels.

**Springs:** And its leaves blow into my pond, and make cozy places for bugs!

**Glo:** And I rest on its branches when the night is long.

**Narrator:** An old oak tree can be home to hundreds of different kinds of creatures. Birds and squirrels, moths
and beetles, and many more.

**Sprout:** So the old oak was never by itself at all.

**Glo:** Never. It looks after all of them. And they look after it, too.

**Glo:** The jays and squirrels bury its acorns all over the meadow. And some of the acorns they forget grow into
new oak trees.

**Narrator:** It's true. Jays and squirrels hide acorns to eat later. And the ones they never come back for can
grow into new oaks.

**Sprout:** So maybe... a squirrel planted me?

**Springs:** And forgot you! Lucky us!

*Sprout looks up at the big tree for a long moment. Its seedling leaves lift.*

**Sprout:** The old oak isn't a squirrel, or a bird, or a frog. But they all belong with it.

**Glo:** That's my secret. You don't have to be the same as your friends. You just look after each other.

*Cut.*

## 6. The Forest Keepers (night, about 90 s)

*Closer, at the foot of the old oak: Sprout, Springs and Digs sit among the roots with gaps between them. Glo hovers
just beside them. The peepers sing far away.*

**Sprout:** I want to look after everybody. Like the old oak does.

**Springs:** Then I'll look after the pond!

**Digs:** And I'll look after the ground. Down underneath, where nobody sees.

**Glo:** And I'll keep watch over the night. Not by myself any more.

**Sprout:** And I'll look after the little growing things. Like me.

**Glo:** Then we're keepers. Keepers of the forest.

**Springs:** Forest Keepers!

**Digs:** I like it.

**Springs:** Can we start right now?

**Glo:** Tomorrow. Tonight, you sleep. I'll be right here.

**Digs:** I'll just take a little nap. Moles take lots of little naps.

**Springs:** I'm not sleepy at all. Not even a... little... bit.

*Springs' eyes close. Sprout's seedling leaves fold down, slowly.*

**Narrator:** And that is how the Forest Keepers began. On a spring night, under the old oak, Sprout found out that
you don't have to be the same as your friends to belong with them. You just have to look after each other.

*The theme comes up. The four, small under the big tree and the stars. Hold. End sign: what should the Forest
Keepers do next?*

## Facts and sources

- Frogs resting underwater all winter, breathing through their skin: [Nature Conservancy of Canada](https://www.natureconservancy.ca/en/blog/archive/how-species-survive-the.html),
  [Three Rivers Parks](https://www.threeriversparks.org/blog/how-do-frogs-survive-winter). True of leopard frogs and
  green frogs, so the line says "some frogs".
- Moles have very poor eyesight and find food by touch (Eimer's organs on the snout) and smell:
  [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/mole-sniffs-world-stereo),
  [Better Planet Education](https://betterplaneteducation.org.uk/factsheets/mole-mole-habits). Moles don't hibernate
  and sleep in short bouts, hence "lots of little naps".
- Oaks support more life than any other North American tree, including hundreds of species of insects, plus birds
  and mammals: [UC ANR](https://ucanr.edu/node/115444), [UGA Extension](https://site.extension.uga.edu/camdenanr/?p=969).
  The line says "hundreds of different kinds" and gives no exact count.
- Oak roots mostly spread out wide near the surface rather than straight down, hence "they spread out, and out".
- Jays and squirrels cache acorns, and the ones never recovered can sprout into oaks. This is how oaks spread.
- Acorns of the red oak group wait over winter under the leaves and sprout in spring when the ground warms. White oak
  acorns sprout in autumn, so the line says "some acorns".
- Spring peepers: tiny frogs whose loud night chorus is a sign of spring.
